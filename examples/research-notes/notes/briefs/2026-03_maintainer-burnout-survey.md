# Community survey report: "Most critical packages have one tired human"

**Date:** 2026-03-27
**Source:** open-source maintainer survey write-up (fictional sample)

## TL;DR

- Of 400 surveyed maintainers, about 60% are unpaid for their maintenance work (survey-reported).
- Nearly half have considered quitting in the last year; triage load is the top reason, not code.
- Corporate users file issues far more often than they fund or contribute fixes.
- Donation platforms help at the margin but rarely cover even one part-time salary.

## Their Argument

The report frames maintenance as invisible infrastructure labor.
Downstream companies treat popular libraries like utilities but pay nothing, and the people holding them up absorb support load, security reports, and entitled issue threads in their evenings.

The authors argue that tips and sponsorship buttons are a mismatch for the problem.
Maintenance needs predictable funding tied to usage, and the current model rewards new projects with attention while starving the ones everyone already depends on.

## Implications

- Concrete evidence that the voluntary funding model is not working for critical dependencies.
- Triage burden is a better-targeted problem than "pay maintainers more" in the abstract.
- Worth tracking whether usage-based funding proposals get real adoption.
