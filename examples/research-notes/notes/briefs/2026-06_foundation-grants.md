# Open-source summit panel: "Grants fund launches, not Tuesdays"

**Date:** 2026-06-18
**Source:** conference panel with three foundation program managers (fictional sample)

## TL;DR

- Most foundation grants are project-based and time-limited, typically 6 to 12 months.
- Maintenance work (releases, triage, dependency updates) is hard to fit into grant deliverables.
- Panelists estimated fewer than 1 in 10 applicants are funded (panel-reported, unverified).
- Security-focused funds were the one area with growing budgets.

## Their Argument

The panel agreed that grant structures reward novelty.
Reviewers want milestones, and "keep the lights on for another year" does not read as a milestone, so maintainers relabel routine work as new features to get funded.

Security was the exception.
After a string of supply-chain incidents, funders found it easier to justify audits and hardening work, but panelists worried that this funds one-off fixes while leaving the chronic maintainer shortage untouched.

## Implications

- Further evidence that open source funding mechanisms mismatch maintenance reality.
- Security framing is currently the most fundable angle for maintenance work.
- The gap between grant cycles and ongoing upkeep is a recurring pattern worth naming.
