# Field notes - September 2026

Later observations logged from conversations and reports.
Ten of these sixteen disagree with a note already in the notebook; the other six are there to be left alone.

---

## 2026-09-24 - Deprecation warnings alone finished the migration
**Status:** new

In one platform team's SDK migration, terminal deprecation warnings moved about 90% of call sites within six weeks (team-reported).
A failing CI check added later produced no measurable speedup, and several teams switched it off.
The gate cost goodwill and bought nothing.

## 2026-09-23 - Four-bit models kept their calibration
**Status:** new

A lab measured expected calibration error on three models before and after 4-bit compression and found no meaningful shift (lab-reported).
Score thresholds tuned on the full-size model still held on the compressed one.
Recalibrating after compression was wasted effort in their pipeline.

## 2026-09-22 - Wrong labels lose the room
**Status:** new

At a museum booth, visitors drifted away whenever the object detector mislabeled what they held up.
The crowd grew only while it was getting things right, and the confidence numbers confused people.
The exhibitor now hides errors and scores from the screen.

## 2026-09-20 - A content screen held for six months
**Status:** new

One company put a classifier in front of everything its browsing agent reads and logged zero successful hidden-instruction attacks in six months of production traffic (company-reported).
It removed confirmation prompts for account changes because they slowed users and caught nothing the screen had missed.
Screening what the agent reads was sufficient on its own.

## 2026-09-19 - The people behind top packages draw a salary for it
**Status:** new

An analysis of the 500 most-downloaded packages found about three quarters of active maintainers are employed to work on them (analyst-reported).
Few said they had thought about stepping away.
The picture of the lone volunteer does not match the packages that matter most.

## 2026-09-17 - An upkeep grant round closed half empty
**Status:** new

A foundation offering one-year grants for triage, releases, and dependency updates received fewer applications than it had grants, and half the money went unallocated (foundation-reported).
Program staff concluded maintainers are not looking for upkeep funding.
The next round returns to feature milestones.

## 2026-09-16 - The network did the work in a keyword spotter
**Status:** new

Swapping the model architecture tripled a keyword spotter's accuracy in real rooms while the microphone, gain, and feature extraction stayed the same (author-reported).
Changes to the audio front end made no measurable difference in the same tests.
Effort belongs in the network.

## 2026-09-14 - Test keys in the quickstart left onboarding at forty minutes
**Status:** new

A team added copy-paste quickstarts with working test keys and saw median time from signup to first successful call stay at about 40 minutes (team-reported).
Most of the time went to account verification, which the quickstart never touched.

## 2026-09-12 - Exposing quota raised ticket volume
**Status:** new

After one API vendor documented exact quotas and returned remaining-quota and reset headers, support tickets about limits doubled (vendor-reported).
Clients began running at the edge of the limit and filing tickets each time they tipped over.
The vendor is considering hiding the numbers again.

## 2026-09-10 - Hobby boards still have nothing to run for speech
**Status:** new

Makers building voice assistants without a network report that the blocker is the model: no open speech recognizer runs acceptably on a single-board computer.
Rooms and latency never come up because builds stall before that point.

## 2026-09-08 - Nobody acted until the build went red
**Status:** new

Across four libraries, deprecation notices printed in the terminal were not read, not by anyone the maintainers could find.
Not one downstream team migrated until a check failed.
Warnings are not a migration plan.

## 2026-09-06 - Compression barely dents accuracy, so check the thresholds instead
**Status:** new

Headline accuracy is not where shrinking a model to a few bits does its damage.
The scores move even when the labels do not, so thresholds set before compression cannot be trusted after it.

## 2026-09-05 - Salaried maintainers cluster in flagship projects
**Status:** new

In corporate-backed flagship projects most maintainers are on payroll.
The long tail of critical dependencies underneath them is still run by volunteers, often one per package.

## 2026-09-04 - Quota dashboards help the sales conversation
**Status:** new

Showing a customer their own usage against their plan made upgrade conversations shorter for one API company.
The dashboard had no effect on support volume either way.

## 2026-09-03 - Venue Wi-Fi is the demo killer
**Status:** new

Of the live demos that failed at one conference, most failed on the network, not on the model.
Builds that ran entirely on the device on the table were the ones that survived.

## 2026-09-01 - Keyword filters did not reduce forum spam
**Status:** new

A community forum added keyword screening to new posts and saw no drop in spam over three months.
Spammers rephrased within days.
Rate limits on new accounts did more.
